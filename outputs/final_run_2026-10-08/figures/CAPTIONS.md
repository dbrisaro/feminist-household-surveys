# Figure captions

The figures of the paper, with the paper's numbering. Generated by `scripts/llm/final_run_figures.py`; the figures are in this folder.

## Figure 1 (`fig1_female_heads_by_unit.png`)

Female-headed households per territorial unit in the microdata of the twelve surveys. Each point is one unit of the most disaggregated territorial variable released in each file (named in parentheses) with at least one sampled household in the quarter; the box spans the first to third quartile and the vertical line marks the median. Counts are unweighted and refer to the same file and quarter as Table 4, excluding 315 British households with no household reference person recorded and 1,929 Chilean households with no single main provider recorded. The horizontal axis is logarithmic, so units with no female-headed household are not drawn but are included in the quartiles and counted at the right of each panel. The dashed line marks 60 cases, the minimum below which INE Chile does not publish an estimate, applied here to all countries as a common reference.

## Figure 2 (`fig2_results_by_country.png`)

Results of the thirteen ranked indicators by country. Countries are ordered by the number of framework elements satisfied (Table 5). A dot marks the five cells in which the three repetitions did not agree (Section 4.2.6).

## Figure 3 (`fig3_unpaid_work_exclusions.png`)

How unpaid domestic and care work enters each questionnaire. For each criterion of subdimension 2, the cell shows whether the instrument measures the item or, if not, how the documentation treats it, by explicit exclusion, by placing it in another category (typically a reason for inactivity), or by not mentioning it. The criterion list d2_i2 is not shown because its elements are only recorded as true or false. A dot marks the cells in which the repetitions did not agree on the answer or on the type of exclusion.

## Figure 4 (`fig4_household_elements.png`)

Elements of subdimension 4 by the number of instruments that capture them. Elements are the components of the criterion lists d4_i3 to d4_i6 (Table 2).

## Figure B1 (`figB1_tokens_by_source.png`)

Tokens in each country's corpus by document type (C questionnaire, M methodological documentation, G interviewer manual or guide), counted with the Gemini 3.6 Flash tokenizer. The number at the end of each bar is the country total. This is the size of the corpus and not what was billed, since each call receives only the document types its indicator draws on.

## Figure B2 (`figB2_run_summary.png`)

Run summary by country, with the actual cost in US dollars, the share of input served from cache, the calls belonging to indicators split into parts, the failed parts or repetitions and the non-unanimous votes. A failed part or repetition is a response that could not be read as a complete assessment.

## Figure D1 (`figD1_stability.png`)

Stability of the classification by indicator and country. The upper panel shows the voted indicators, where stability is the share of repetitions that agree with the vote, averaged over elements for criterion lists. The lower panel shows the record indicators, which are not voted, where the measure is the mean word-set similarity between the texts of the three repetitions, so low values can come from wording rather than content. Both measures run from 0 to 1 but measure different things. The definition of headship was not assessed in Paraguay, whose corpus has neither methodological documents nor a manual.

## Figure E1 (`figE1_exclusion_types.png`)

Type of exclusion for each unmet criterion, by indicator and country. An exclusion is explicit when the text states that the item is excluded, treated as another category when the text mentions the item but places it elsewhere (typically as a reason for inactivity), and not mentioned when the text does not refer to it. Met cells are marked as met, and criterion lists are not shown because they carry no exclusion type. A dot marks the cells in which the repetitions did not agree on the answer or on the type of exclusion.
